Federated bodies of work, held in common

Any participant in the community of practice can share their body of work as a project. Projects add to, and form, the net work: federated bodies of work with common peers of the cooperative — stewards, members, and guests.

A project is the open entry point. A venture is the same body of work read as an external relationship; a program, the same work read as an internal one. Any body of work can be all three at once. Stewards designate programs and ventures; any participant may share a project.

This is the alpha shell of the open-world application: a second door onto the cooperative's Common Information System. It invents no permission — what you can see here is exactly what the record already lets you see. Guests read this page; members sign in to read the record they are entitled to.

Stamps proposed

A stamp is a prepaid service credit. You would buy or be granted a block of them, and the cooperative would meter what it carries for you against a published rate card, the way a post office franks a letter. The meter prices carriage and never content: a stamp does not know what your letter says.

Your stamp standing is computed from your own acts and is never comparative.

None of this exists. No stamp has been issued, nothing is for sale, and no price has been set. The schema this section would read is drafted across four migrations and none of them are applied. What they wait on is not code: whether the cooperative hosts members' mail at all, whether the board grants the one carve-out that lets a member see a number about themselves, and whether counsel reads Colorado's gift-card escheat statute as blocking the sale of a credit. Those are the humans' to answer. The working record is techne.coop issue #196 and the migration stop card that sits in front of 0031 through 0034.

Rate card unpriced

No coefficient is filled in, and that is deliberate rather than unfinished. A rate travels in the payload of an instrument the board adopts, never in code, and every figure published so far is a placeholder. The first honest audit of what delivery actually costs is the founding act, not a number typed here.
